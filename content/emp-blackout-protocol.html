Before you buy: blackout basics that don't need a guide

Blackout Protocol focuses on keeping power and electronics going. It helps to have the free fundamentals in place first, since they matter in any outage, short or long:

  • Battery-powered carbon monoxide and smoke alarms. Never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors or in a garage.
  • A small set of flashlights or lanterns with spare batteries, plus a battery or hand-crank radio for news.
  • Stored drinking water (US emergency agencies suggest at least one gallon per person per day) and food that doesn't need cooking.
  • A plan for staying warm in one closed-off room if the outage happens in winter.
  • Surge protectors for sensitive electronics, and knowing where your main breaker is, so you can switch large appliances off and avoid a surge when power returns.

If you already have those covered and want a more structured plan for protecting power and electronics, that's where a dedicated guide like this one fits in.
