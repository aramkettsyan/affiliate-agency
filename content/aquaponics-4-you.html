Aquaponics, which means raising fish and growing plants in the same recirculating water, is one of the more interesting corners of home gardening. Aquaponics 4 You is a guide to building your own system. Here's what the official sales page says you get, which claims we'd treat with caution, and who it suits. We based the details below on the vendor's page rather than on building a system from the guide ourselves.

What is Aquaponics 4 You?

It's a build-it-yourself guide to home aquaponics, in which fish waste feeds the plants and the plants help clean the water for the fish. The vendor says it comes as a PDF ebook with diagrams, explanations, and step-by-step instructions, along with step-by-step video instructions. The page says it covers small home systems and can be scaled up, and it specifically describes floating-raft growing, including where to place floats, which material to use, and how to plant seedlings.

What's included

  • The main guide: a digital PDF with instructions, diagrams, and common mistakes to avoid.
  • Video instructions: advertised as step-by-step instructions for building a homemade system. The page also mentions a DVD sent to your home, so check whether that still applies before you order.
  • Bonus ebooks: Organic Gardening for Beginners, Herbs for Health and Home, A Guide to Flower Gardening, Worm Farming, Guide to Organic Cooking, and Eating Healthy. The vendor values these at $300, but that's the seller's own figure.

Price and guarantee

When we last checked the official page, it listed a sale price of $37 as a one-time fee, and it also displayed a discount offer. Prices can change, so confirm the current one before you buy. The vendor advertises a 60-day money-back guarantee.

Who is it best for?

Home gardeners and DIYers who are new to aquaponics and want one structured set of build instructions, rather than piecing a design together from forum posts. If you've already built a working system, much of this may be familiar.

Things to keep in mind

  • Treat the yield claims with caution. The page promises up to ten times the plants, growth up to twice as fast, and up to 70% less energy. We can't verify those figures, and a first home system shouldn't be budgeted around them.
  • "Almost completely automated" oversells it. A real system needs regular feeding, water testing, and pump and plumbing checks, and it can fail quickly if something stops working. The fish depend on you every day.
  • The cost doesn't stop at the guide. You supply the tanks, pumps, plumbing, grow beds, fish, and electricity. Price those out locally first.
  • Check climate and local rules. We couldn't confirm how far the guide goes on cold-weather operation, so plan for that yourself if you get hard winters. Rules on keeping some fish species also vary by location.
  • Read the marketing with some skepticism. The sales page is hype-heavy and also dismisses hydroponics in sweeping terms. We can't verify its testimonials or its farm examples, and a commercial-scale farm isn't a realistic benchmark for a backyard setup.

The bottom line

If you want a low-cost starting point for building a first home system, and you can look past the sales language, Aquaponics 4 You looks like a reasonable option, and the 60-day guarantee limits the risk.
