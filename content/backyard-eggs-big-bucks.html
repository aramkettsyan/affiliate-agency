Backyard Eggs = Big Bucks is a DIY guide for homeowners thinking about keeping a small flock of hens. This review sets out what we can and can't confirm about it, and what a new keeper should weigh before spending money on either a guide or the birds themselves.

What is Backyard Eggs = Big Bucks?

It's a guide aimed at homeowners who want to raise backyard chickens for fresh eggs, covering coop setup and the basics of keeping a small flock. We couldn't find a detailed, independent breakdown of its chapters, so we aren't going to list contents we can't verify. Check the official page below for the current description before you buy.

Who is it best for?

It's best suited to complete beginners who are curious about a small flock and would like a starting point in one place. If you already keep hens or you've got a specific problem to solve, such as an illness in your flock, you're likely better served by a poultry vet, your local extension office, or a detailed reference on that topic.

A note on the "big bucks" name

The title leans on egg prices, so set your expectations before you start. A few hens can be a real source of fresh eggs, but feed, bedding, the coop, predator protection, and vet care all cost money, and hens lay less with age and during the fall and winter. Treat any savings or income as something to work out with your own local costs, not a promise. If you're hoping to sell eggs, check your local rules first, too.

Before you buy any chicken guide

  • Check that your city, county, or homeowners' association allows hens, and whether there are limits on flock size or coop placement.
  • Plan your space. We've broken down how much room chickens need in a coop.
  • Expect a seasonal slowdown. Our guide on why chickens stop laying in the fall explains why this is usually normal.
  • Consider who will look after the birds daily, including when you travel.
