Self-sufficiency and homesteading have a huge, devoted audience. Self Sufficient Backyard is a guide aimed at producing more of your own food, water, and power at home. Here's an honest look at what the official page says it covers, who it suits, and what to weigh before buying.

What is Self Sufficient Backyard?

It's a homesteading book from the same publisher behind The Lost Ways, written by Ron and Johanna. Their official page describes them as "back to the land" homesteaders from Nova Scotia. They say they have lived off the grid for about 40 years. Ron describes himself as a former city person, born and raised in Philadelphia. The book is pitched as a collection of practical backyard projects. The sales page advertises "100+" DIY projects with how-to illustrations.

What does it cover?

According to the official page, topics include:

  • Power: a hybrid off-grid electricity system and a hot water system.
  • Water: water collection, independent water sources, and automatic irrigation.
  • Food growing: a year-round greenhouse, straw bale gardening, companion planting, dwarf fruit trees, a medicinal garden, and a 12-month planting calendar.
  • Soil: composting and a simple mason-jar soil test.
  • Food storage: living without refrigeration, an earth refrigerator, and survival foods.
  • Small livestock: a chicken coop and egg/protein tips.
  • Planning: choosing a homestead location.

The page also lists three bonuses, which it labels as digital products: The Aquaponic Gardener, DIY Projects from the 1900s, and a guide on where free land can still be found in the US. Bonus offers on sales pages can change, so check what's included when you order.

Who is it best for?

Homesteaders, preppers, and gardeners who want one broad overview of food, water, and energy projects rather than a deep manual on a single topic. It suits people who are starting to plan their yard as a system. For example, a compost pile feeding vegetable beds, rain collection feeding irrigation, and a few chickens.

The good

  • Written by authors who say they have decades of first-hand off-grid experience.
  • Very broad scope, covering power, water, food growing, storage, and chickens in one place.
  • Project-oriented with illustrations, according to the sales page.
  • 60-day money-back guarantee, as stated on the official page and handled through ClickBank.

Things to keep in mind

  • Breadth comes at a cost. A book covering this many topics can't go as deep on any one of them as a dedicated manual on, say, solar wiring or greenhouse growing.
  • The official page doesn't list a page count or file format, and the price shown is a time-limited discount. Confirm the format and current price at checkout.
  • Results depend on your land, climate, budget, and local rules. Larger projects such as off-grid power or water systems may need permits or a professional.
  • Upsells may appear after purchase.

The bottom line

Self Sufficient Backyard is a reasonable pick if you want a wide-ranging, project-based starting point from long-time homesteaders and you don't mind that it's broad rather than deep. The 60-day guarantee makes it low-risk to try. See the official page below for current pricing and details.
