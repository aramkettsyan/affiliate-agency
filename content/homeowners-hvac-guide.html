What a homeowner's HVAC reference can and can't do

A written guide like this is most useful for the jobs homeowners can safely handle themselves: knowing when to change filters, keeping outdoor units and vents clear, checking thermostat settings, clearing a condensate drain line, reading a furnace status light, and recognizing which symptoms are normal and which mean a call to a technician. That knowledge alone can head off a lot of "no heat" and "no cooling" emergency visits that turn out to be a dirty filter or a tripped breaker.

What no book can replace is a licensed technician for gas, refrigerant and electrical work. Anything involving gas valves, burners, refrigerant charge or the heat exchanger should be left to a professional, and every home with a fuel-burning appliance should have working carbon monoxide alarms.
