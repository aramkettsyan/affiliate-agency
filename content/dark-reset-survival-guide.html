What a good blackout plan should cover

Whatever guide you use, a practical grid-down plan should answer a few basic questions before the power goes out. Use this list to judge Dark Reset, or any preparedness guide, against what you actually need:

  • Heat: which room you'll keep warm, how you'll seal it off, and which heat sources are safe to use indoors without carbon monoxide risk.
  • Water: how much drinking water you have stored (a common rule of thumb from US emergency agencies is at least one gallon per person per day) and how you'd treat more.
  • Food: what's in the fridge and freezer, how long it stays safe without power, and what you can eat without cooking.
  • Light and communication: flashlights, spare batteries, a battery or hand-crank radio, and a way to charge phones.
  • Health: a basic first-aid kit, a supply of any prescription medicines, and a plan for anyone who depends on powered medical equipment.
  • Safety: working battery-powered smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and knowing where your water shutoff is.

Most of these basics cost little and can be set up in an afternoon. A paid guide is most useful if it helps you turn that list into a written plan your household will actually follow.
