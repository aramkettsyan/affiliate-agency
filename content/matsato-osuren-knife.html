The Matsato Osuren is a compact 6.3-inch chef's knife sold direct to home cooks. This review covers what the maker actually publishes about it, what those numbers mean in the kitchen, and who should look elsewhere. We haven't run our own long-term cutting test, so the specifications below come from Matsato's June 2026 press release, which is a paid placement, and should be read that way.

What is the Matsato Osuren Knife?

The Osuren is the newer of Matsato's two main chef's knives, positioned as the more precise option next to the original Matsato Classic. Matsato describes it as a Japanese stainless steel blade with a taper-ground edge and a dual-wood handle.

Published specifications

  • Blade length: 6.3 inches (16 cm)
  • Total length: 10.6 inches (27 cm)
  • Weight: 180 g (0.40 lb)
  • Blade steel: Japanese stainless steel (no specific grade named)
  • Hardness: 54–56 HRC
  • Edge angle: 12°–14° (taper grind)
  • Handle: dual-wood

What those numbers mean in practice

A 12–14 degree edge is a fine angle, which generally means less resistance through tomatoes and thin slices, but a thin edge also needs more care around bones and hard squash. A hardness of 54–56 HRC is on the softer side compared with many higher-end Japanese knives, which are often harder. Softer steel tends to be easier to sharpen at home and less prone to chipping, but it usually needs honing or sharpening more often. In short, expect an easy-to-maintain knife, not one that holds its edge for an unusually long time.

At 6.3 inches the blade is also shorter than the 8-inch chef's knife many cooks are used to. That can feel nimble in a small kitchen, but it's less suited to large cabbages, melons, or big roasts.

Who is it best for?

It makes the most sense for home cooks who want a light, smaller everyday knife and are comfortable with basic upkeep. It's a weaker fit if you want a specific named steel, a longer blade, or a knife you'll rarely need to sharpen.

The good

  • Light and compact, with published dimensions and hardness
  • A fine edge angle suited to precise slicing
  • Matsato says it can be sharpened at home with a whetstone or sharpener
  • A 60-day return policy, per the maker

Things to keep in mind

  • The exact steel grade isn't named, which makes direct comparison harder
  • It's hand-wash only; Matsato says never to put it in the dishwasher
  • It's a physical product, and the maker quotes 7–9 business days for typical US delivery and 10–18 for international orders
  • Marketing language runs high around this brand, so check current details and prices before buying

The bottom line

The Matsato Osuren looks like a reasonable light everyday knife for someone who wants a smaller blade and doesn't mind regular sharpening.
