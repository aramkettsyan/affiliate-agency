What any treehouse plan should help you with

We haven't verified the full table of contents, so use this as a checklist when you look over the official page or the guide itself. A useful treehouse plan should cover:

  • Choosing a healthy tree and checking it can carry the load (and when to call an arborist)
  • How the platform attaches to the tree. A few large, purpose-made bolts are much gentler on a tree than lots of nails, and the structure should leave room for the trunk to grow and sway (see our guide on attaching a treehouse without damaging the tree)
  • Beam and joist sizes, plus a materials list you can take to the lumber yard
  • Railings, ladder or stairs, and safe heights for kids
  • Local permit and setback rules, which vary by town and HOA

What a guide can't do for you

No ebook can inspect your particular tree. If the tree has dead limbs, fungus, cavities or recent root damage, get it checked by a certified arborist before you build, whatever plan you use.
