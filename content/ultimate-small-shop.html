Setting up a woodworking workshop can get expensive fast, and most of us don't have a spare two-car garage to put it in. UltimateSmallShop is a guide aimed at building a complete, functional small workshop on a tight budget and in a tight space. Here's what the official page says it includes, what we could and couldn't verify, and who it actually suits.

What is UltimateSmallShop?

The Ultimate Small Shop guide is written by Ralph Chapman, who describes himself on the official page as a woodworker of roughly two decades and a former mentor to woodworking students. His pitch is personal: after losing his dedicated shop in 2008, he rebuilt in much smaller spaces and on a much smaller budget. The vendor describes the product as a 246-page illustrated book, sold digitally through ClickBank.

What's inside: the six modules

According to the official page, the guide is organized into six modules:

  • Tool selection: a hand-tool shopping list aimed at under $500, and a combined power and hand-tool list aimed at under $1,000.
  • Space selection: pros and cons of common workshop locations such as a garage, basement, attic, or apartment corner.
  • Shop layouts: layouts for spaces of about 10' x 10' or smaller, plus ideas like mobile bases so machines can share floor space.
  • Electricity, lighting and sound proofing: wiring and lighting considerations and ways to keep noise down, which matters in shared homes.
  • Heating, cooling, ventilation and dust: keeping the space usable year-round and managing dust.
  • Safety: fire risks, trip hazards from cables, and a safety checklist.

The budget and space claims, in context

The headline promise is a complete shop for under $1,000, with layouts for spaces as small as 8' x 8'. The author says his own shop cost him about $600. Those are the vendor's figures, not ours, and your real cost will depend heavily on which tools you already own, whether you buy new or used, and local prices. The page also makes marketing claims about large discounts versus big-box stores through "secret" suppliers; we haven't been able to verify those, so treat them as a possibility rather than a promise.

Who is it best for?

Beginner and hobbyist woodworkers who want a structured plan for building out a first workshop without overspending, and anyone working in a small garage bay, basement corner, or shed who needs a smart layout. If you already have a well-equipped shop, most of the tool-buying advice will be familiar territory.

The good

  • Budget-focused approach, with tool lists organized around spending caps.
  • Covers the less glamorous parts of a shop that beginners often forget: electrical, lighting, noise, dust and climate.
  • Practical layout and storage guidance specifically for small spaces.
  • 60-day money-back guarantee through ClickBank.

Things to keep in mind

  • It's a guide, not tools; you still need to buy the tools it recommends.
  • Discount and savings figures on the sales page are the vendor's claims and weren't independently verified.
  • Upsells may appear after purchase.
  • Electrical work in particular should follow local code; when in doubt, use a licensed electrician.
